How the comparison works

Choosing a setup

Each candidate trains on all reconstructable history before the latest 30 days. Those 30 days are withheld as one comparison period, with the same eligible tracks for every candidate. Training needs at least 14 earlier days and 100 modeled tracks; comparison needs 50 first-play tracks across seven active days.

The worker checks daily, requiring 30 distinct freshly played tracks after the previous comparison. A switch needs a supported improvement of at least five prediction matches and two percentage points. Uncertain results, ties and incomplete comparisons keep the current setup. Each comparison corrects for testing multiple alternatives.

Training and restoring

After selection, the chosen setup trains on all current data. Candidate fits run privately in bounded steps while the last published model remains available. A switch saves the exact previous vectors and configuration for restoration; restoring pauses automatic selection.

These scores measure first-play prediction accuracy. They do not establish better recommendation menus, album choices or enjoyment. Historical menu experiments and prospective forecast measurements remain available in their original reports.
