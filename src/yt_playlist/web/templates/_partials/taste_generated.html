Generated playlists

The four mixes on Home’s Listen tab find music in different ways. Their titles reflect the music in each mix.

  1. 01

    Choose a music group

    The model groups tracks by genre, era, and sound. Your library, mix choices, and recent listening help decide which groups to draw from.

  2. 02

    Find the tracks

    Each mix picks tracks using one of the four approaches below.

  3. 03

    Make room for variety

    Leave out hidden music, avoid repeats between mixes, and limit tracks by the same artist or album.

  4. 04

    Order the tracks

    The list is run through a DJ model to produce a coherent progression.

What your mix choices teach the model

When you play the mix, we use that choice to train the model. Future mixes lean toward groups you choose, while giving others a chance.

Each row is a group of similar tracks. A group can span several genres, or share a genre with another group but differ in era or sound. The percentages describe the music inside it; your saved genre weights are separate.

More offers usually narrow the bar as the model gets a clearer picture. A group can have a low score and a short bar: the model has good evidence that you rarely choose it.

Scores start with one selection and one pass to soften the first few results. Playing the same mix again adds another selection, so selections can outnumber offers. Sound descriptions compare measured tracks with your library; each shows how many tracks have that measurement.

Source weights

These sliders only affect the familiar-music mix. It uses Deep cuts plus Taste matches, with Playlist neighbors standing in until taste matching is ready.

What the numbers mean
×1 is the default. ×2 gives a source about twice as many spots on the shortlist as ×1. The final mix still follows genre choices and artist limits.
When you give feedback
“More” adds 15% to a source’s weight. “Less”, dismissing, or snoozing takes 15% off. “Already know it” just hides the track.
Saving and resetting
Sliders save when you let go. Changes apply at the next recommendation refresh. Reset takes a source back to ×1. Left alone, weights drift toward ×1: for example, ×2 becomes ×1.5 after {{ '%.0f'|format(viz.lane_weight_halflife_days) }} days, even with learning off. Older weights with no saved date stay put.
